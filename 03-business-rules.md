## A. Customer Management

| Rule ID | Rule Name | Description | Business Justification | Priority | Dependencies | Exceptions | Future Consideration |
|---------|-----------|-------------|----------------------|----------|--------------|------------|---------------------|
| A-001 | Customer Registration Requirement | Every customer must register with a valid Tanzanian mobile phone number. Registration requires: full name, phone number, and M-Pesa or Mixx account linked to that number. | Phone number is the primary identifier and payment gateway in Tanzania. Mobile money accounts are linked to phone numbers, making this the most practical KYC method. | C | None | None | Allow email-based registration when expanding internationally. |
| A-002 | Minimum Age Requirement | Customers must be at least 18 years old to register. | Minors cannot enter binding contracts for service fees. Legal liability and payment authorisation require adult status. | H | A-001 | If Tanzanian law changes age of majority; if parental/guardian accounts are introduced. | Introduce family accounts with guardian-managed sub-accounts for minors (Future). |
| A-003 | Phone Verification | The customer's phone number must be verified via SMS one-time password (OTP) or USSD callback before account activation. | Prevents fake accounts, reduces fraud, ensures the customer owns the number used for mobile money. | C | A-001 | None. Verification is mandatory for all accounts. | Add biometric or national ID verification for high-value accounts (V2). |
| A-004 | Account Deactivation — Customer Request | Customers may deactivate their account at any time. Deactivation takes effect immediately. Pending orders must be cancelled or completed before deactivation. | Regulatory requirement and good UX. Gives customers control over their data and account. | H | A-001, D-101 (Order Expiry) | If the customer has outstanding unresolved disputes, deactivation is blocked until disputes are resolved. | Self-service data export before deactivation (Future). |
| A-005 | Account Suspension — Platform Initiated | The platform may suspend a customer account if: (a) fraudulent activity is detected, (b) multiple payment failures, (c) abusive behaviour toward shoppers, (d) violation of terms of service. | Protects shoppers and platform integrity. A single abusive customer can damage shopper trust and supply. | C | I-101 (Fraud Detection) | Suspension is appealable through a formal process within 7 days. | Implement tiered suspension (soft block vs hard block) based on severity (V1). |
| A-006 | Maximum Active Orders per Customer | A customer may have no more than 3 active (placed but not yet delivered) orders at any time. | Prevents abuse of platform resources, reduces risk of payment default, ensures manageable load. | M | D-101 | Customers with verified corporate accounts or premium status may have higher limits (V2). | Dynamic limits based on customer history, rating, and payment reliability (V2). |
| A-007 | Customer Communication Preference | Customers must specify their preferred language (English, Swahili) and notification channel (push, SMS, in-app) during registration. Preferences can be changed at any time. | Tanzania is bilingual. SMS is essential for feature phone users. Respecting preferences improves UX and reduces opt-out. | H | A-001 | Emergency notifications (order issues, security) are always sent via SMS regardless of preference. | Add more languages (French for neighbouring countries, Future). |
| A-008 | Dormant Account Cleanup | Customer accounts with no login or order activity for 12 consecutive months shall be flagged as dormant. Dormant accounts are subject to data minimisation (personal data archived, phone number released). | Reduces data storage costs, limits breach exposure, complies with emerging data protection principles. | L | None | Accounts with positive wallet balance cannot be archived until funds are claimed. | Send notification before archiving; implement re-activation flow. |
| A-009 | Customer Data Retention | Customer personal data shall be retained for the duration of the account's active status plus 3 years after deactivation. Transactional data shall be retained for 7 years for tax/legal purposes. | Tanzanian tax law requires 5-7 years of financial record retention. Privacy law (when enacted) will require deletion after purpose ends. | M | A-004 | If a legal hold (court order, active dispute) exists, data is retained until hold is released. | Implement automated data purging workflows (V1). |
| A-010 | Customer Order History Access | Customers shall be able to access their complete order history for the past 12 months from the app. Older order data shall be available on request within 7 days. | Customers need visibility for budgeting, reordering, and dispute reference. 12 months is standard for mobile apps. | M | None | None | Infinite scroll with lazy-load from archive (V1). |

## B. Shopper Management

| Rule ID | Rule Name | Description | Business Justification | Priority | Dependencies | Exceptions | Future Consideration |
|---------|-----------|-------------|----------------------|----------|--------------|------------|---------------------|
| B-001 | Shopper Registration — Minimum Requirements | To register as a shopper, an individual must: (a) be at least 18 years old, (b) possess a valid Tanzanian government-issued ID (national ID, voter ID, or passport), (c) own a smartphone with GPS capability, (d) have a valid mobile money account verified via OTP, (e) have **reliable access to transport** (may own, rent, or borrow — ownership not required), (f) provide emergency contact information, (g) agree to the Shopper Terms & Code of Conduct. Phone number must be OTP-verified. | Minimum viability requirements for service delivery. Smartphone with GPS is essential for real-time tracking. "Reliable access to transport" rather than ownership reflects Tanzanian reality where many riders rent or borrow motorcycles. Emergency contact and Code of Conduct establish accountability from day one. | C | A-001 | For areas with low smartphone penetration, consider assisted registration via platform agents (V2). | Accept regional IDs when expanding across East Africa (Future). |
| B-002 | Identity Verification with Periodic Re-Verification | Shopper identity must be verified through: (a) photo of government-issued ID submitted via app, (b) facial recognition or live photo matching the ID, (c) manual review by platform admin before approval. The platform SHALL support periodic re-verification triggered by: (a) long inactive period (> 90 days), (b) suspicious activity detected, (c) significant account changes (phone number, mobile money account), (d) routine periodic checks (recommended: every 12 months). | Trust is the platform's core value proposition. Unverified shoppers undermine the entire business model. Periodic re-verification ensures that identity remains valid over time and reduces fraud from account takeover or identity changes. | C | B-001 | If automated facial recognition fails (poor photo quality, lighting), manual review is the fallback. Re-verification may be expedited for active shoppers with clean records. | Biometric verification via third-party KYC providers (V2). |
| B-003 | Background Verification — Tanzanian Reality | The platform shall implement a Tanzania-appropriate background verification process: (a) **Mandatory** — National ID verification (via NIDA where available), Police Clearance Certificate (required for shoppers, especially as the platform scales), mobile number OTP verification, mobile money account name-matching. (b) **As available** — Integration with government digital identity APIs when they become accessible. Applicants with verified convictions for violent crimes, theft, or fraud shall be automatically disqualified. | Tanzania does not have a widely available automated nationwide background-check service that startups can easily integrate with. Mandating such a check would block registration for most applicants. A pragmatic approach uses available verification methods (NIDA, Police Clearance Certificate, mobile money matching) while leaving room for future government API integration. | C | B-002 | If Police Clearance Certificate is not yet obtainable by the applicant (backlog, distance to issuing office), provisional approval may be granted with a 60-day deadline to submit the certificate. Shoppers handling high-value orders (> 200,000 TZS) must have full clearance before acceptance. | Government API integration for real-time identity verification (Future). |
| B-004 | Shopper Onboarding Training | Approved shoppers must complete a mandatory onboarding programme covering: (a) app usage and navigation, (b) order acceptance workflow, (c) shopping best practices, (d) product substitution handling, (e) customer communication and professionalism, (f) product quality assessment (fresh produce, expiry dates, damaged packaging), (g) fraud prevention awareness, (h) safety during deliveries, (i) emergency procedures, (j) privacy and data protection. | Comprehensive training directly reduces operational errors, support load, and preventable disputes. Topics like product quality, fraud prevention, and privacy are especially important for customer trust and legal compliance. | H | B-003 | Shoppers with verifiable delivery experience from other platforms may skip certain modules at admin discretion (case-by-case). | Gamified onboarding with assessment quiz and certification (V1). |
| B-005 | Shopper Status — Active/Inactive | A shopper is considered **available** when: (a) they manually set status to "Active" (Go Online), (b) the app is connected and transmitting a regular heartbeat signal, (c) GPS data is being received at the expected frequency (see C-001). A shopper is considered **unavailable** when: (a) they manually set status to "Inactive" (Go Offline), (b) the app disconnects and no heartbeat is received for a configured timeout (recommended: 2 minutes), (c) there is no GPS AND no app heartbeat for the configured timeout. | Relying solely on GPS to determine availability is unreliable — temporary GPS loss (tunnel, indoor) should not mark a shopper offline. Combining manual status + GPS + app heartbeat creates a more accurate picture of true availability. | C | C-001 | The app heartbeat timeout is configurable (recommended: 2 minutes). Shoppers cannot be offered orders while Inactive, even if GPS is transmitting (app in background but shopper not working). | Allow scheduled availability ("I'll be available from 5pm") (V1). |
| B-006 | Minimum Availability Requirement (Deferred) | No minimum order requirement SHALL be applied during the MVP / early growth phase. The platform MAY introduce configurable activity expectations per zone once the platform reaches a stable operating volume, based on actual demand in each zone rather than arbitrary thresholds. | During MVP, demand may be low. A shopper should not be penalised because the platform hasn't yet generated enough orders to sustain them. Minimum requirements are only fair once there is consistent demand to meet them. Premature thresholds discourage early supply. | M | B-005 | Shoppers on documented leave are exempt when thresholds are eventually introduced. Shoppers in zones with insufficient demand are not penalised. | Tiered system: Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum based on orders/month (V1). |
| B-007 | Shopper Suspension Grounds | A shopper may be suspended for: (a) acceptance rate below 40% over a rolling 30-day period, (b) completion rate below 90% (once a shopper accepts, customers expect fulfillment), (c) customer rating below 4.0 after sufficient completed orders (new shoppers with fewer than 20 ratings are not judged on this threshold), (d) substantiated fraud or theft, (e) abusive behaviour toward customers or staff, (f) failure to maintain required documents. Penalties follow progressive discipline: 1st quality violation — warning, 2nd — coaching or retraining, 3rd — temporary suspension, 4th — final review. Permanent removal is reserved for repeated serious misconduct or fraud only. | Higher thresholds (90% completion, 4.0 rating) raise the quality bar, which directly improves customer experience. New shoppers need a grace period before being judged on ratings. Progressive discipline gives honest shoppers room to recover from mistakes. | H | H-001, H-003, H-004, B-011 | Serious violations (fraud, theft, abuse) skip progressive discipline and result in immediate suspension pending investigation. If investigation confirms the violation, permanent removal applies. | Remediation programme — suspended shoppers can retake training (V1). |
| B-008 | Shopper Performance Tiers | Shoppers shall be classified into performance tiers based on: order volume, acceptance rate, completion rate, customer rating, and on-time delivery percentage. Tiers SHALL influence: (a) assignment priority in the Assignment Score (C-011), (b) access to premium/high-value orders, (c) early access to new features, (d) recognition badges displayed in the app. Tiers SHALL NOT directly change shopper earnings — the shopping fee (F-005) already compensates based on order size and complexity. New shoppers start at base tier for the first 30 days regardless of performance. | Performance tiers incentivise quality without creating large income disparities. Changing pay based on tier can feel arbitrary and unfair — the shopping fee already scales with effort. Priority access and recognition reward good performance without financial distortion. | M | B-006, B-007, C-011 | New shoppers start at base tier for first 30 days. Shoppers in zones with insufficient demand to meet tier thresholds are not penalised. | Publish tier criteria transparently in shopper app (V1). |
| B-009 | Shopper Transport Verification with Zone Eligibility | Shoppers using motorised transport (motorcycle, car) must provide proof of valid vehicle registration, driving licence, and insurance and must maintain valid documentation. Cyclists and those using public transport need only valid ID. Transport type SHALL determine eligible delivery zones: (a) Walking — nearby neighbourhoods only (short-distance), (b) Bicycle — short-to-medium distance deliveries, (c) Motorcycle — citywide delivery capability, (d) Car — citywide + bulky/high-value orders. The assignment engine (C-011) uses transport type as a factor in eligibility. | Legal compliance and safety assurance. Transport type-based zone eligibility makes the assignment engine smarter — a pedestrian should not be offered a 10 km delivery, and a car offers capacity for bulky orders that a motorcycle cannot handle. | H | B-001, C-011 | Shoppers may update their transport type in their profile (subject to re-verification of documents). Shoppers using public transport are treated as walking for zone eligibility but may mark their current zone manually. | Offer platform-negotiated insurance for shoppers (V2). Electric vehicle incentives (Future). |
| B-010 | Concurrent Order Limit | A shopper may accept only one order at a time. A new order may be offered only after the current order is marked as "Delivered" or "Cancelled." | Prevents multi-tasking that degrades service quality, causes delays, and increases error rates. Single-order focus ensures quality. | C | D-101 | V2 may introduce batch delivery (2-3 orders on same route) for experienced shoppers. | Batch delivery rules to be defined separately (V2). |
| B-011 | Acceptance Rate Tracking | The platform shall track each shopper's acceptance rate as: (Accepted Orders ÷ Offered Orders) × 100 over a rolling 30-day period. The following SHALL be excluded from acceptance rate calculation: (a) platform outages, (b) confirmed GPS or network failures on the shopper's device, (c) orders withdrawn before the shopper could reasonably respond, (d) offers made while the shopper was in a known low-connectivity area. | Rolling 30 days balances recency with statistical significance. Excluding platform/network failures ensures shoppers are not penalised for events outside their control. Accuracy in this metric is critical because it feeds into the Assignment Score (C-011, 20% weight) and suspension decisions (B-007). | C | B-007, B-008, C-011 | Offers that time out during a platform outage or known network issue are excluded. Shoppers may request manual exclusion of specific offers if they can demonstrate a connectivity issue. | Show shopper their live acceptance rate in-app (V1). |
| B-012 | Shopper Appeal Process | Shoppers have the right to appeal any suspension, tier demotion, or financial penalty. Appeals must be submitted within 7 days. The platform SHALL send an **initial acknowledgment within 24 hours** confirming receipt. A **final decision SHALL be communicated within 5 business days**, unless the case requires external verification (e.g., Police Clearance Certificate validation). | Due process is essential for worker trust and legal defensibility. The 24-hour acknowledgment reassures shoppers that their appeal has been received and is being processed — silence damages trust. | M | B-007 | Appeals involving fraud or safety violations are reviewed at a higher administrative level. Cases requiring external verification may extend beyond 5 business days with periodic status updates to the appellant. | Automated appeals for common suspension types (V2). |
| B-013 | Shopper Working Hours — Per-Zone Configurable | Each service zone SHALL define its own operating hours (default for V1: 06:00-22:00). Shoppers may set their availability within their zone's operating hours. Shoppers cannot accept orders that cannot be completed before the zone's closing time. Zone operating hours may differ: commercial zones may operate later, residential zones may close earlier. Holiday and festive season schedules are configurable per zone. | A single national operating window is too rigid — different zones have different demand patterns. Kariakoo may need to operate until 23:00, while airports may need 24/7 coverage in the future. Making hours configurable per zone now prevents a costly redesign later. | M | B-005, C-007 | Customers may request after-hours delivery for a premium fee where shoppers are available and the zone supports it (V2). During Ramadan or festive seasons, zone hours may be extended independently. | Dynamic hours extension based on real-time demand and shopper availability (V1). |
| B-014 | Shopper Incentive & Recognition Program | The platform SHALL implement a positive incentive programme alongside its quality/penalty systems. Incentives may include: (a) "Top Shopper" badges displayed on profile, (b) priority access to high-value orders, (c) monthly performance bonuses (when financially viable), (d) in-app recognition (leaderboards, achievement milestones), (e) early access to new features, (f) exclusive community events or channels. | The best marketplaces don't rely solely on punishment — they create positive incentives that encourage shoppers to stay active and deliver excellent service. Recognition programmes have low marginal cost but high motivational value. Retaining experienced shoppers is critical as the platform grows. | M | B-008, C-011 | Bonus programmes are only introduced when unit economics support them. Recognition badges are always free to implement. | Performance-based insurance premium discounts (V2). Fintech rewards — lower loan interest rates for top shoppers (Future). |

## C. GPS & Assignment Logic

| Rule ID | Rule Name | Description | Business Justification | Priority | Dependencies | Exceptions | Future Consideration |
|---------|-----------|-------------|----------------------|----------|--------------|------------|---------------------|
| C-001 | Shopper GPS Ping Frequency — Tiered by State | The shopper app shall transmit GPS coordinates at frequencies based on shopper state: (a) Online, waiting for orders — every 30 seconds, (b) Request offered (countdown active) — every 10 seconds, (c) Accepted, traveling to market — every 5 seconds, (d) Shopping inside market — every 15 seconds, (e) Delivering to customer — every 5 seconds, (f) Offline — no GPS transmission. | State-based frequency balances battery life and data usage with accuracy needs. Higher frequency during travel (5s) enables accurate road distance calculations for assignment. Lower frequency while waiting (30s) preserves battery. | C | B-005 | If GPS signal is lost, last known location is used with a timestamp. Battery-saver mode may override frequencies during low-battery states (reducing all by 2x). | Adaptive ping frequency based on speed/movement (V1). |
| C-002 | Distance Calculation — Hybrid Haversine + Road Distance | The platform shall use a two-step distance calculation: Step 1 — Use Haversine (straight-line) formula to quickly identify the nearest 20 Active shoppers to the market location. Step 2 — Calculate actual road distance (via Maps API) for those 20 shoppers. The road distance SHALL be used for final ranking, delivery fee calculation, and offer radius validation. | Haversine is a fast pre-filter that avoids expensive API calls for distant shoppers. Road distance for the top candidates ensures fairness and accuracy where it matters most. This is the industry standard approach used by major dispatch systems. | C | C-001, B-005 | If the Maps API is unavailable, fall back to Haversine distance × 1.5 (empirical road-to-straight ratio for urban areas). If fewer than 20 shoppers are in the zone, calculate road distance for all of them. | Real-time traffic-aware distance calculation (V2). |
| C-003 | 30-Second Acceptance Window | An offered shopper has 30 seconds to accept an order. If the shopper does not respond within 30 seconds, the system treats it as a declined offer. | Balances giving shopper time to review with customer expectation of fast assignment. Industry standard (DoorDash, Uber). | C | C-002 | The clock starts when the offer notification is delivered to the shopper's device, not when it is sent. Network delay is not counted. | Show countdown timer prominently in shopper app with sound/vibration (MVP). |
| C-004 | Offer Cascade — Maximum 3 Minutes | If a shopper declines or times out, the system immediately offers the order to the next nearest shopper (ranked by Assignment Score, see C-011). This cascade continues until: (a) a shopper accepts, or (b) 3 minutes have elapsed since the first offer. Each shopper gets the full 30-second acceptance window. | 3 minutes is sufficient to cycle through 6+ shoppers (30s each), covering most realistic scenarios. Shorter cascade means faster customer feedback if no shoppers are available. Industry practice favours quick fallback over long cycling. | C | C-002, C-003, C-011 | If no shopper accepts within 3 minutes, the order is flagged as "Unassigned" and the customer is notified with options: (a) increase delivery fee, (b) expand assignment radius, (c) cancel the order. If the customer selects (a) or (b), the cascade restarts with updated parameters. | Simultaneous offers to multiple shoppers in low-supply areas (V2). |
| C-005 | Maximum Offer Radius — Per-Zone Configurable | Every zone SHALL define its own Maximum Assignment Radius (road distance). Examples: Kariakoo zone — 6 km, Mbezi zone — 12 km, Airport zone — 8 km. These values SHALL be configured by administrators based on local geography and market research, not hard-coded as fixed values. Shoppers outside this radius shall not receive offers for orders in that zone. | A single radius does not fit all of Dar es Salaam — dense central areas have shorter viable delivery distances than sprawling suburbs. Per-zone configurability allows the platform to adapt to each neighbourhood's geography and shopper density. | H | C-002, C-007 | Radius may be dynamically expanded during low supply (see C-006). Radius may shrink during peak hours to maintain fast delivery times. | AI-based dynamic radius based on real-time traffic and shopper density (V1). |
| C-006 | Dynamic Supply Management — Supply/Demand Ratio | The platform shall monitor supply vs. demand per zone using the ratio: Pending Orders ÷ Available Shoppers. When the ratio exceeds a configurable threshold (recommended: 5.0), the system may: (a) expand the Maximum Assignment Radius by up to 50% for that zone, (b) increase the delivery fee for customers in that zone, (c) display extended delivery time estimates, (d) send demand alerts to shoppers in adjacent zones. Example: 20 pending orders ÷ 10 shoppers = 2.0 (healthy). 150 pending ÷ 20 shoppers = 7.5 (low supply — activate interventions). | A supply/demand ratio is simpler and more meaningful than arbitrary density thresholds. It directly reflects the customer experience (how many shoppers per waiting order) and triggers intervention only when needed. | M | C-005 | Customer must acknowledge extended time/increased fee before the order is placed. Interventions are automatically reversed when the ratio drops back below threshold. | Predictive supply rebalancing — send demand alerts to shoppers in adjacent zones (V2). |
| C-007 | Geographic Coverage Zones with Zone Policies | The platform shall define geographic zones (neighbourhood-level) with boundaries. Each zone SHALL have: name, center coordinates, radius, operating status (active/inactive/limited), current shopper count, Maximum Assignment Radius (see C-005), and a **Zone Policy** defining: base delivery fee, surge rules, operating hours, and service fee tier. Zones can be independently activated, deactivated, or placed in limited service. | Zone policies enable differentiated operations per neighbourhood — high-density zones can have lower fees and stricter SLAs, while developing zones can have wider radii and surge pricing. This is essential for phased city rollouts. | H | C-002 | Zones can be temporarily deactivated for special events, holidays, emergencies, or during phased rollout. | Automated zone activation based on demand prediction (V2). |
| C-008 | Fairness Through Weighted Assignment Score | Fairness SHALL NOT be enforced as a hard limit. Instead, the assignment algorithm SHALL use a weighted Assignment Score (see C-011) where distance receives 40% weight, performance 30%, availability 20%, and recent workload 10%. This naturally distributes orders across shoppers — shoppers who have received fewer orders recently get a higher workload score, increasing their chance at the next offer. | Hard limits (e.g., "no shopper receives more than 20%") are brittle — they can cause suboptimal assignments during low supply. A weighted score naturally balances efficiency with fairness without artificial caps. The workload component rises as a shopper receives fewer orders, creating organic rotation. | M | C-002, C-011 | In extreme low-supply scenarios (ratio > 10, see C-006), the workload weight may be reduced to prioritise order fulfillment speed. | ML-based dynamic weight tuning (V2). |
| C-009 | Customer Location Privacy — Show General Area, Hide Exact Address | The customer's exact delivery address SHALL only be revealed to the shopper after the order is accepted. However, before acceptance, the shopper SHALL see the **general area**: neighbourhood name (e.g., "Mikocheni"), approximate distance from market (e.g., "6 km"), and estimated earnings. | Hiding the exact address prevents cherry-picking by neighbourhood and protects privacy. Showing the general area gives the shopper enough context to assess whether the order is worth their time — especially important for large/heavy orders where distance matters. Balances shopper needs with customer privacy. | H | C-002 | None. The exact address is NEVER shown before acceptance. The general area display is always approximate (±200m) to prevent reverse-geolocation. | Customer chooses how much detail to show before acceptance (V2 preference setting). |
| C-010 | GPS Spoofing Detection — Multi-Pattern | The platform shall monitor shopper GPS data for anomalies indicative of spoofing: (a) impossible speed between consecutive pings (> 200 km/h), (b) sudden jumps to unrelated coordinates (teleportation), (c) static location while moving (no GPS change for > 2 minutes while status indicates travel), (d) impossible routes (e.g., GPS shows location in water, restricted area, or non-road), (e) GPS signal quality anomalies (sudden switch from low accuracy to perfect accuracy at a suspicious moment), (f) repeated pattern of GPS loss during critical moments (e.g., always losing signal just before delivery). Flagged events are logged for manual review. | Multiple detection patterns catch different spoofing techniques. A single rule (e.g., speed only) is easy to bypass. Layered detection increases deterrence. False positives are reduced by requiring anomaly patterns rather than single events. | H | C-001 | Temporary GPS signal loss is not flagged if the app reports a "GPS lost" status with sensor data (accelerometer/gyroscope) confirming movement. Single anomalous pings are ignored; patterns of 3+ anomalies within 10 minutes trigger a review. | Machine learning model for spoofing pattern detection (V1). |
| C-011 | Intelligent Assignment Score | Instead of offering orders purely by nearest distance, the platform SHALL calculate an **Assignment Score** for each eligible shopper. The shopper with the highest score receives the offer first. Assignment Score = Distance (35%) + Acceptance Rate (20%) + Completion Rate (15%) + Customer Rating (10%) + Current Workload (10%) + Recent Activity (5%) + Zone Priority (5%). Distance is calculated using the hybrid method (C-002). The remaining factors are calculated over a rolling 30-day window. | A multi-factor assignment score prevents the "nearest shopper always wins" problem, which leads to unfair workload distribution and ignores quality differences. Shoppers with higher reliability (acceptance rate, completion rate, rating) earn priority, creating an incentive system for quality service. Workload and recent activity components ensure natural rotation. | C | C-001, C-002, C-008, H-003, B-011 | New shoppers (first 30 days) have a modified score where distance is weighted at 50% and performance factors are derived from the limited available data. If an Assignment Score tie occurs, the shopper who has waited longer in Active status is selected. | ML-based dynamic weight optimisation using historical outcomes (V2). |

## D. Order Lifecycle

| Rule ID | Rule Name | Description | Business Justification | Priority | Dependencies | Exceptions | Future Consideration |
|---------|-----------|-------------|----------------------|----------|--------------|------------|---------------------|
| D-001 | Order Creation — Minimum Requirements | An order requires: (a) a valid customer account, (b) at least one item with name and quantity, (c) a market/source location, (d) a delivery location, (e) a delivery time preference (ASAP or scheduled), (f) a **shopping preference**: Cheapest Available, Best Quality, or Balanced (price + quality). The shopping preference guides the shopper in their purchasing decisions throughout the order. | Ensures the shopper has sufficient information to fulfill the order. The shopping preference reduces the need for constant customer contact during shopping — the shopper knows whether to prioritise price, quality, or a balance. | C | A-001 | Scheduled orders require a time at least 2 hours in the future. | Voice-based item entry (AI) and photo-based order creation (Future). |
| D-002 | Item Entry — Manual with Optional Fields | For MVP, customers shall manually enter item names and quantities. The platform shall also provide optional fields per item: (a) preferred brand, (b) preferred quantity/unit (kg, pieces, litres, etc.), (c) maximum acceptable price (customer sets a ceiling). The platform shall provide predictive text suggestions based on common items. | Manual entry is pragmatic for MVP since no vendor inventory exists. Per-item optional fields prevent misunderstandings — a request for "rice" without specifying brand, unit, or max price leads to mismatched expectations. Maximum price protects customers from overpriced substitutions. | C | D-001 | None in MVP. V1 may introduce item templates and saved lists. | Barcode scanning (V2), vendor inventory integration (V2), AI photo recognition (Future). |
| D-003 | Order Pricing — Customer Visibility | Before submitting an order, the customer shall see a detailed price breakdown: (a) estimated item cost (based on shopper purchase), (b) platform service fee (tiered per F-001), (c) delivery fee (road distance-based per F-002), (d) total estimated cost. Final cost may vary based on actual prices at the market. Approval from the customer is required if the final cost exceeds the estimate by MORE THAN 10% OR more than 10,000 TZS, whichever occurs first. | Transparency principle — customers must consent to costs before order placement. A combined threshold (10% OR 10,000 TZS) protects both small and large orders: a 5,000 TZS order that spikes to 6,000 TZS is a 20% increase (caught by the 10% rule), while a 200,000 TZS order that rises to 215,000 TZS is only 7.5% but is a 15,000 TZS gap (caught by the 10,000 TZS rule). | C | D-001, F-001, F-002 | If the customer does not respond to the approval request within 5 minutes, the order is processed at the original estimate and any overshoot is covered by the platform as a "price guarantee" (up to the threshold). Customers with high Trust Scores (F-012) may have a higher auto-approval threshold. | Real-time price feed from vendor integration eliminates variance entirely (V2). |
| D-004 | Order Status Lifecycle — Expanded State Machine | Orders shall follow a defined state machine with the following statuses and transitions: **Created** → **Awaiting Payment Verification** → **Queued for Assignment** → **Offered** → **Accepted** → **Travelling to Market** → **Shopping** → **Shopping Complete** → **Receipt Verified** → **In Delivery** → **Delivered** → **Completed** → **Archived**. > **Design Note — Shopper Trust Score:** Rather than relying on star ratings alone as the definitive measure of shopper quality, the platform should internally combine subjective ratings with objective operational indicators into a **Shopper Trust Score**. The **Assignment Score (C-011)** should use this Trust Score as an input (currently represented as "Customer Rating 10%"), providing a more robust measure that is resistant to biased reviews, rating manipulation, and isolated incidents.

| K-004 | Network Failure — Order Continuation | If a shopper loses network connectivity during an active order: (a) the app continues to operate in offline mode with cached data, (b) GPS points are queued and transmitted when connectivity resumes, (c) status changes are queued locally, (d) critical actions (mark delivered) are queued for batch sync. | Network interruptions are common in Tanzania. Offline resilience prevents order abandonment due to temporary connectivity loss. | H | C-001, D-004 | If connectivity is not restored within 30 minutes, the system attempts to reach the shopper via SMS for status. After 60 minutes, the order is escalated to support. | Full offline mode with local storage of orders and route maps (V2). |
| K-005 | Natural Disaster / Civil Unrest | In the event of a natural disaster, civil unrest, or government-imposed curfew, the platform administrator may: (a) suspend operations in affected zones, (b) cancel all active orders in affected zones with full refunds, (c) release all pre-authorisation holds, (d) pay shoppers for time invested up to the point of suspension. | Safety of customers, shoppers, and staff takes precedence over operations. Clear authority to suspend and protocol for doing so. | C | C-007, G-005 | The suspension decision is made by the platform administrator. Partial suspensions (certain zones only) are possible. | Automated disaster alerts integration (weather services, government alerts) (Future). |
| K-006 | Order Item Discrepancy — Dispute | If the customer claims that items delivered differ from what was ordered/receipt (wrong items, missing items, damaged items): (a) customer submits a dispute through the app within 2 hours of delivery, (b) customer uploads photo evidence of the issue, (c) support reviews the evidence and chat logs, (d) if shopper error is found, the refund is processed per G-007. | Structured dispute process ensures fair handling and prevents emotional escalation. Evidence-based resolution. | H | E-007, G-007, J-001 | If evidence is inconclusive, goodwill compensation is discretionary based on investigation findings (per G-009 discretionary goodwill policy) — not an automatic 50%. | AI-assisted dispute resolution — photo comparison, receipt verification (V2). |
| K-007 | Customer Dispute — Shopper Behaviour | If a customer reports inappropriate shopper behaviour (harassment, aggression, discrimination): (a) the shopper is immediately suspended pending investigation, (b) support reviews chat logs, rating history, and any available recordings, (c) a decision is made within 48 hours, (d) if confirmed, permanent shopper deactivation. | Behavioural complaints require swift action to protect customers and the platform's reputation. Zero-tolerance for abuse. | C | J-001, J-007 | If the complaint is found to be false/malicious, the customer receives a warning. Repeat false complainants are subject to account suspension. | Body camera / dashcam integration for shoppers (Future). |
| K-008 | Lost Items — Post-Delivery | If a customer reports an item missing from their delivery after the inspection window (E-007): the claim is generally denied as the inspection window has passed. However, if the customer provides compelling evidence (video of unpacking, weight discrepancy), a discretionary review may be conducted. Decisions SHALL be evidence-based, not status-based — no automatic special treatment for high-volume customers. | The inspection window exists to prevent indefinite liability. Strict enforcement is necessary for operational clarity. Exceptions must be justified by evidence, not customer status. | M | E-007 | In exceptional cases with compelling evidence, support may issue a one-time courtesy credit at their discretion. | Smart packaging with weight verification at dispatch (Future). |

Monitoring enables continuous improvement and early warning of degradation. | H | D-004, C-003 | SLA breaches trigger automated alerts to operations admin. Persistent SLA breaches (> 10% above target for 7 days) trigger a review. | Machine learning SLA prediction — anticipate and prevent breaches (V2). |
| L-006 | Platform Maintenance Window | Scheduled maintenance shall occur between 03:00 and 05:00 East Africa Time (EAT). | L-007 | Terms of Service — Acceptance | All users (customers and shoppers) must accept the current Terms of Service and Privacy Policy at registration. OTP verification for major changes provides clear consent evidence. | C | A-001, B-001 | Minor changes (typo corrections, formatting) may not require re-acceptance. | L-008 | Dispute Triage — Automated | Incoming disputes shall be triaged automatically: (a) straightforward cases (clear evidence, under 10,000 TZS) → automated resolution per policy, (b) standard cases → assigned to support agent queue, (c) complex cases (high value, contradictory evidence, pattern concerns) → escalated to operations admin. | Improves support efficiency by routing cases to the appropriate resolution path automatically. Reduces manual sorting overhead. | M | G-006 through G-009 | Any party may request escalation to a human reviewer regardless of automated resolution. Escalated cases are reviewed by an operations admin. | AI-powered dispute resolution recommendation engine (V2). |
| L-009 | Business Continuity — Data Backup | All platform data shall be backed up: (a) database: hourly incremental + daily full backup, (b) media (photos): daily backup, (c) backups stored in a separate geographic location from primary infrastructure. | Data loss is existential for a marketplace platform. Backup redundancy protects against infrastructure failure, human error, and malicious attacks. | C | None | Backup retention: daily backups retained for 30 days, weekly for 6 months, monthly for 2 years. | Active-active multi-region failover (V2). |
| L-010 | Platform Metrics Dashboard | The admin dashboard shall display: (a) live orders map (ongoing orders with shopper locations), (b) daily/weekly/monthly GMV, (c) active shopper count + utilisation rate, (d) order fulfillment rate, (e) average assignment time, (f) dispute rate, (g) new user registrations. | Operations need real-time visibility into platform health. Metric-driven management is essential for scaling. | H | L-005 | Metrics refresh every 30 seconds for live data, every hour for aggregated data. Historical data available for 12 months. | Predictive analytics dashboard with trend forecasting (V2). |
